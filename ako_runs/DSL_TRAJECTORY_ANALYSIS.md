## 1. TL;DR

**The single most important finding: gap *size* tells you nothing about gap *cause*. You must trace the lever, not read the number.** The four ops that diverge beyond the noise band, ordered by raw spread:

| Rank (by spread) | Op | Spread (max/min) | Cause | Legitimacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `scatter` | **1.52×** (10 → 6.56; ~1.62× if the 10 cap is lifted) | Triton has no block-scoped shared atomics; cuda_noptx missed the high-occupancy *partial*-fusion form | **Mixed** — intrinsic vs Triton, artifact vs cuda_noptx, contested-bench vs tilelang |
| 2 | `group_norm` | **1.48×** (1.354 → 0.917) — *but see stall caveat below* | cuda_noptx kept the 3× traffic model; never tried the off-diagonal L2-resident chunk pipeline | **Artifact** (search) |
| 3 | `layer_norm` | **1.32×** (2.16 → 1.64) | cuda_noptx never tried per-row L2 residency | **Artifact** (search) |
| 4 | `gather` | **1.15×** (1.508 → 1.311) | coalescing win found in winning form once; bottom pair deformed/missed it | **Artifact** (search) |

`scatter` is the **largest raw *tabulated* spread** on the board (and clearly largest once the `10` display cap is lifted to ~10.65×). *(Strictly, `group_norm`'s stall-corrected steady-state spread ~1.53× edges scatter's tabulated 1.52× — but scatter's true spread is larger, so the ranking-by-tabulated-value is the honest, self-consistent comparison.)* Of the four divergences, **three are dominated by search artifacts** — one DSL (`cuda_noptx`) failing to *find* a PTX-free traffic-reduction lever it could fully *express* — and only *one slice* of one op (scatter → Triton) is a genuine intrinsic ceiling. If you read the table as a capability ranking you would draw exactly the wrong conclusion about `cuda_noptx`.

> **`group_norm` column caveat (applies to all four cells):** every group_norm log flags a fixed, kernel-independent **Trial-1 `cudaMalloc`(8.59 GB) page-commit stall** (~289–300 ms on the first timed trial, hitting whichever kernel is timed first — the reference eats it too). It drags *all four* reported means below steady state. Steady-state mins are ≈ **1.0× (cuda_noptx), 1.44× (unlimited), 1.50× (tilelang), 1.53× (triton)**. So cuda_noptx's tabulated **0.917× is ~parity with torch at the 3× model**, not a real loss, and the true winner cluster is ~1.5×, not 1.28–1.35×. The *relative* story (cuda_noptx alone stuck at 3× traffic) is unaffected; the gap magnitude is somewhat inflated by comparing one stall-dragged mean to others.

Everything else:

- **Five pure activations (relu/sigmoid/hardsigmoid/elu/gelu) + lstm are ONE finding, not six.** Memory-bound-elementwise and cuDNN-floored regimes are **DSL-agnostic**: every DSL parks within ~2–3% at a shared physical floor. This is where the study's noise band is calibrated (~±2.5–3%), and it is the reference against which every "divergence" must be judged.
- **The widest lever-set does not win by its exclusive levers.** `cuda_unlimited` tops the most ops, but its signature inline-PTX streaming stores are *systematically null* under HBM saturation (proven by its own plain-vs-`st.global.cs` A/Bs on relu, sigmoid, hardsigmoid, elu, gelu, swish, cumsum, group_norm, layer_norm). It wins by **searching deepest** and finding *DSL-agnostic algorithmic* levers (L2 residency, shared-mem staging, consecutive-group MLP, register-direct ILP). Its **only** clean lever-set-intrinsic win is `scatter` (block-scoped shared atomics).
- **`cuda_noptx` is a strict PTX-less subset of `cuda_unlimited`, and its PTX gap is almost never load-bearing.** Where it loses big (layer_norm, group_norm) the decisive lever needs *no* PTX. Its real weakness is a behavioral one: it over-trusts its port-time traffic model and declares "at floor" before questioning whether the traffic itself is reducible.
- **`tilelang`'s losses are the only ones with a real codegen-intrinsic component** (can't pin a consecutive-group per-thread layout → swish; `T.Pipelined` silently breaks a loop-carried scan carry → cumsum; JIT launch tax on ~20 µs kernels → gather/scatter), but even these cost only single-digit-percent, and its cooperative-grid primitive makes it a *co-winner* on reduction-normalization.
- **Same decision, opposite outcome (regime-conditioned):** mapping "one row/element unit to a block" is *optimal* for `layer_norm` — one block per row (64 blocks) is the *fastest* variant because each thread issues ~16k *independent* loads that saturate HBM even at 6% occupancy — and *starving* for `scatter`, where the same 64-block mapping leaves compute-light one-element-per-thread work unable to fill 142 SMs (cold-DRAM-bound). The grid-mapping lever is not good or bad in the abstract; the regime decides.

## 2. The causal framework

A DSL's speedup on an op is set by exactly one thing:

> **performance = the best lever that lies in (EXPRESSIBLE in the DSL) ∩ (FOUND by the AKO search) ∩ (RELEVANT to the op's regime).**

The three sets do different jobs, and a trajectory difference only *legitimately explains* a performance difference when it maps onto the first set (or a forced-slow primitive). Concretely:

- **RELEVANT (regime)** decides whether *any* lever exists above the port. In a memory-bound-elementwise regime the relevant set is `{maximum-width coalesced copy}` and nothing else — so the intersection is a single point every DSL reaches, and the DSL is irrelevant. In a reduction-normalization regime the relevant set contains an *algorithmic* lever (cut 3× HBM traffic to 2× by keeping a row/chunk L2-resident) that is worth 1.3–2×, so the regime *admits* divergence.
- **EXPRESSIBLE (lever-set)** is the only source of a **legitimate, permanent** gap. Two shapes:
  - *Not expressible at all* — e.g. Triton exposes no block-scoped shared-memory atomics, so the winning scatter kernel is **out of its set** no matter how long it searches. This is a true ceiling.
  - *Expressible only in a forced-slow form* — e.g. tilelang's `T.Parallel` auto-vectorizes and will not let you pin an explicit consecutive-group per-thread layout, or its `T.Pipelined` desyncs a loop-carried carry and must be abandoned. Codegen/dtype traps are ceilings too, just softer ones.
- **FOUND (search path)** is where the honesty discipline bites. When a lever is *in* a DSL's expressible set but the search never tried it (or tried it in the wrong form), the resulting gap is an **artifact**. It reflects the AKO trajectory, not the DSL. Reading it as a capability ranking is the error the prior GAP_ANALYSIS caught: `cuda_noptx` missing L2 residency on layer_norm/group_norm is search luck, not a hardware or DSL limit.

**Decision rule used throughout this document.** For every divergence, ask: *is the winner's decisive lever expressible in the loser?*
- **No** → intrinsic (lever-set ceiling or forced-slow codegen). The gap is real and permanent.
- **Yes, but the loser never found it (or found it deformed)** → search-path artifact. The gap is a trajectory accident; the loser's number is **not** its ceiling.
- Ablation beats narrative: if a DSL's *own* A/B shows its exclusive lever ties a generic one, that lever is **non-causal** even if a trajectory summary credits it.

That last clause matters because the widest-lever-set DSL keeps *shipping* a lever (PTX streaming stores) that its own measurements show buys nothing. Crediting it would manufacture a phantom intrinsic advantage.

## 3. The four DSL trajectory personalities

### 3.1 `triton` — the autotune + cache-residency specialist (high-level, reopens false floors)

Triton's trajectory signature is two-fold: it drives an offline `tl.autotune` sweep (BLOCK × num_warps × num_stages) on *every* op, and it steers L2/L1 retention with per-load/store `eviction_policy` (`evict_last`/`evict_first`) hints. This is an **ergonomics** edge, not an exclusive one: the CUDA siblings have their own cache hints (cuda_noptx: `__ldcs`/`__stcs`/`__stwt` streaming/evict-first; cuda_unlimited emits `st.global.cs`/`ld.global.nc` and could express evict-*last* via raw PTX). What Triton uniquely offers is **evict-LAST *retention* as a first-class, per-load hint** — and it is the only DSL that actually *won* an op with an eviction hint (gather, group_norm). The CUDA siblings reached the same coalescing win by shared-memory staging instead. On memory-bound floors Triton converges in ~2 iterations and reverts every exotic lever as noise. On signal-carrying ops it goes 5–10 iterations deep and, critically, **reopens self-declared rooflines**: both the layer_norm and group_norm logs explicitly overturn a prior "at the 3× traffic floor, done" verdict via a pure-torch pre-probe, then re-climb to the L2-residency win. This makes Triton a *discoverer* of the L2 lever that `cuda_noptx` missed — a search *strength*, and the eviction hint is a genuine expressiveness edge for cashing it in (group_norm **winner** at 1.354×; gather ablation shows `evict_last` is the *sole* ~30% **wall-clock** driver — *ncu: the associated "~97% of HBM peak" figure is retracted, measured 68.6%; the ~30% runtime win stands*).

Its one hard ceiling is real and permanent: **no block-scoped shared-memory atomics**. On scatter this forces its fusion attempt onto a *global* winner buffer, which dies on occupancy (only 64 blocks), capping it at 6.79× against the 10× shared-atomic champion. Secondary soft limits: the autotuner picks the ILP point, so hand-tuned consecutive-group (swish G=6) and register-direct (cumsum EPT=16) micro-levers go unswept — but those are worth <3%, so they cost Triton almost nothing.

### 3.2 `cuda_noptx` — the disciplined plain-CUDA porter (its true ceiling is behavioral, not the PTX gap)

`cuda_noptx` is, by construction, `cuda_unlimited` minus inline PTX. It always ports the verified Triton algorithm, reaches for `float4` + deep per-thread ILP + TPB=256, and confirms floors with revert-everything passes. It books **no exclusive intrinsic win** — every lever it keeps (float4, ILP, TPB, fp32/fp64 accumulators) is DSL-agnostic and available to two siblings.

Its nominal limitation — no inline PTX, restricted to `__ldcs/__stcs/__stwt` intrinsics — is **almost never load-bearing**. Its own sibling's ablations prove store-policy is immaterial at the bandwidth wall (`st.global.cs` ties a plain store on relu, sigmoid, group_norm, ...), and its streaming intrinsics measured null in every op that tried them. Attributing its losses to "no PTX" would be dishonest.

Its **actual** weakness is behavioral and shows up *only* in reduction-normalization: it accepts a traffic model adopted at port time and floors against it. Two shades of the same habit: on **layer_norm** it never once mentions L2 residency — its "distinct directions exhausted" list (roofline, split-row occupancy, ILP/MLP, TPB, contiguous-vs-strided, fp64, atomic-vs-direct) literally omits the per-row-fits-L2 direction, so it stops at "3.26 GB / 3-pass / 90% of peak — at floor" without ever asking if the traffic is reducible. On **group_norm** it is subtler and *not* uniquely incurious: it *did* consider L2 residency but only in the **occupancy-throttled fused-persistent form** (rejected via a "8 MB group exceeds shared/registers, would throttle to ~12 blocks" argument) — and never tested the **off-diagonal "many CTAs on a few groups" chunk pipeline** that its three siblings used. In fact all four DSLs initially made the same "3× irreducible" call by probing only the diagonal (one CTA per whole group); the breakthrough (found first in the Triton log) was the off-diagonal re-probe, which cuda_noptx alone never ran. That single habit makes it the lone reduction-normalization loser twice, at 1.64× and (stall-adjusted ~parity) 0.917×, purely by search path. Where it senses headroom on a small latency-bound kernel it is the *deepest* searcher in the pool (gather: 18 directions, an explicit occupancy-vs-ILP frontier table) and lands a strong result (gather 1.327× via NG=4 ILP=16, a raw-C++ ILP route the tile DSL cannot pin) — so the personality is not "shallow," it is "confidently floors within its adopted model."

### 3.3 `cuda_unlimited` — the deepest, most skeptical searcher (widest lever-set, but wins by depth)

`cuda_unlimited` refuses "at floor" verdicts. Its trajectories open with an *empirical* ceiling/traffic-decomposition probe (a 2×float4 copy on swish hitting 835 GB/s to prove MLP headroom; a cold-L2 diagnostic on gather reproducing `bench.py`'s L2 clear), then out-iterate every sibling (8–13 explored iterations per signal op). That depth is the mechanism by which it converts a floored port into a win — and the levers it finds are overwhelmingly **DSL-agnostic**: L2 residency (host-side chunk/row-blocking loop), consecutive-group MLP (swish G=6), shared-mem row staging (gather), register-direct ILP (cumsum EPT=16), fp32 accumulators (group_norm).

Its widest-in-the-pool lever-set (inline PTX: `st.global.cs.v4` streaming stores, `ld.global.nc.v2.s64` vectorized loads, `red.global.max`, packed 64-bit atomics, `cudaFuncSetAttribute` 64 KB shared opt-in) is mostly **along for the ride**. The streaming store is null on ~every op (swish 15.21 vs 15.22 ms; cumsum 10.211 vs 10.215 ms; group_norm cs/cg/plain all ~22.3 ms). Its **one** truly intrinsic win is `scatter`: a packed 64-bit `atomicMax` in *block-scoped shared memory* that Triton structurally cannot express (10× vs Triton's 6.79×).

Limitations are the flip side of its deep-ILP habit: a register-spill cliff hard-caps the sweeps it loves (swish spills at G=7/8; cumsum at EPT≥32 → 771 GB/s at EPT=64), `launch_bounds(256,6)` silently forbids 512-thread blocks (contaminated one A/B until caught), and cooperative-grid launches cap resident blocks so `grid.sync` fusion loses to multi-launch everywhere it tried (layer_norm 3.24 vs 2.84 ms; group_norm 25.7 vs 21.9 ms).

### 3.4 `tilelang` — the JIT tile DSL (effortless floors, distinctive cooperative grid, real dtype/codegen traps)

`tilelang` converges at HBM floors with near-zero manual effort: `T.Parallel`/`T.copy`/`T.cumsum` auto-emit coalesced float4 code, so on elementwise and scan ops it ports, verifies the codegen, reverts its two probes (explicit `T.vectorized`, guard removal) as null, and restores baseline verbatim. Its **distinctive positive capability** is `T.sync_grid`: a cooperative persistent-grid kernel (auto `cudaLaunchCooperativeKernel`) that expresses layer_norm's single-read L2 residency as *one* barrier-synchronized kernel — the exact opposite mechanism to `cuda_unlimited`'s multi-launch loop, converging to the same 2.10× floor. It also has the shared atomics Triton lacks (`T.alloc_shared` + block-scoped `atomic_max`), which put it 2nd on scatter (7.63×, beating Triton).

`tilelang` is the **only** DSL whose losses carry a genuine codegen-intrinsic component:
- **the fp64/AD102 accumulator trap (its most consequential dtype trap, a *porting* characteristic):** the *original* tilelang layer_norm port scored **0.65×** — a 2.28× gap to CUDA's 1.49× — purely because it used **fp64 accumulators for the long per-row reduction**, which run at **1/64 the fp32 rate** on AD102 (consumer-class FP64). Swapping to fp32 at *fixed* 64-block occupancy jumped it 0.65×→**1.61×** (2.5×), while *adding* 128× more blocks (split-row) was *slower* — proving the loss was precision, not occupancy. This is the classic tile-DSL porting trap on this hardware ("reach for fp64 to be safe on a 4.19M-element chain"), and the durable lesson — *keep reduction accumulators in fp32 on AD102 unless a short-chain precision argument truly demands fp64*. (It was fixed pre-deeper-pass; the current committed tilelang layer_norm 2.10× is the L2-residency result on top of the fp32 fix, so this trap does not appear in the final numbers — but it is the single most distinctive tilelang trajectory event and cost the largest transient gap in the study.)
- **swish 2.43× (last):** `T.Parallel` auto-vectorization gives no control to pin the consecutive-group per-thread layout, so the G=6 MLP lever is out of its expressible set — a real (if ~4%) ceiling.
- **cumsum `T.Pipelined` trap:** software-pipelining a loop-carried scalar-carry recurrence silently produces wrong output (max_diff ~16640, CORRECT=False), *removing* pipelining as a scan lever. (Standalone codegen fact — **not** the reason it trails on cumsum; see §5.)
- **Structural taxes:** int16 atomics unsupported (forced int32 shared atomics + int16 only as a global-buffer *traffic* trick on scatter); cooperative-launch grid-size cap forces TH=64 on layer_norm; `T.if_then_else` loads *both* operands (~1 MiB wasted reads on scatter pass-2); JIT ~3.3 µs/launch is negligible on ~20 ms ops but a real drag on ~20 µs kernels (gather, scatter, lstm projection). Its tiled scatter win also carries a **portability caveat** — it hardcodes KS=16/WS=32 and indexes `K//KS`, `W//WS`, so it assumes K,W divide evenly (true here, but not general like the original `T.Parallel(K)`).

Its shared-with-cuda_noptx "no inline PTX" limitation is, as everywhere, immaterial.

## 5. The decisive divergences dissected

### 5.1 `scatter` — the board's only clean intrinsic ceiling, wrapped in a contested-bench artifact
`cuda_unlimited 10× · tilelang 7.63× · triton 6.79× · cuda_noptx 6.56×` — spread 1.52×

All four solve the same *reframed* problem: torch's racy duplicate-index scatter is order-nondeterministic and unscorable, so every DSL targets the **deterministic last-wins** variant (commutative argmax `atomicMax`), where torch's own path is ~16× slower and leaves real headroom. From that shared base they split on **how far kernel fusion could go and where atomics could live** — and this op is where the causal framework earns its keep, because the 1.52× spread decomposes into three *different* causes:

- **vs Triton (intrinsic, permanent).** The champion's decisive lever is a fused block-per-row kernel doing its last-wins argmax with a **packed 64-bit `atomicMax` in shared memory** (`((k+1)<<32 | float_bits(update))`, with a `cudaFuncSetAttribute` 64 KB opt-in), which kills both the memset launch and the uncoalesced gather. Triton **cannot express block-scoped shared atomics at all** — its fusion attempt was forced onto a *global* winner buffer and died on occupancy (64 blocks). This is the single clean lever-set-intrinsic result on the entire board: no amount of search moves Triton past it. (Notably the PTX `red.global.max`/`st.global.cs` path lived only in the *reverted* 2-pass global baseline — so even here the win is *shared atomics*, not PTX.)
- **vs cuda_noptx (artifact — but not "pure," and not the full-fusion lever).** `cuda_noptx` **has** shared atomics, and it never built the block-per-row shared packed-64 structure. But be precise about *which* miss is the artifact. Its non-pursuit of the champion's **full-fusion** kernel rests on the *same* cold-bench reasoning this document credits as legitimate for tilelang (below): a 64-block fused kernel is cold-DRAM-starved. So faulting it for not building full fusion would contradict crediting tilelang's identical verdict. The lever it *genuinely, uncontestedly* missed is tilelang's **partial fusion** — shared-atomic pass-1 flushing to global + a separate **high-occupancy** 2048-block pass-2 gather + an int16 WIN traffic trick (7.63×) — which is high-occupancy and therefore *not* excused by the cold model. That is the real search artifact; the full-fusion gap is a defensible bench-regime disagreement, not a discovery failure.
- **vs tilelang (contested / regime).** This is the honesty crux and it is **not** a footnote. `cuda_unlimited` justified full fusion by asserting the ~12 MB working set is L2-resident and "not bandwidth-bound." **`cuda_noptx` explicitly identified that as a measurement artifact** — `bench.py` clears 256 MB of L2 before each trial, so kernels run *cold/DRAM-bound* — and **`tilelang` independently agreed and therefore ranked the very same full-fusion lever *worse***, measuring the 64-block fused kernel at ~290 GB/s (cold-starved) and choosing *partial* fusion (shared-atomic pass-1 flushing to global + a separate high-occupancy 2048-block pass-2 gather) plus an int16 WIN-buffer traffic trick → 7.63×. So **two sibling DSLs reached the opposite empirical verdict on the winner's decisive move.** The 10-vs-7.63 gap is therefore *regime-contested*, not a clean ceiling — under the cold bench the "L2-resident" premise that motivates full fusion is exactly what the losers dispute. *(ncu splits the sub-claims: **"not bandwidth-bound" is CONFIRMED** — cold, all DSLs sit at 40–47% of peak, not the ~89% of a saturated op. But **"L2-resident" is REFUTED under the bench** — cold L2-hit for the fused kernel is only 31%; the ~101% hit appears only in a warm counterfactual, and bench.py clears L2 every trial, so the fused kernel is fast because it is **traffic-light + latency-light, not L2-resident**. And the honest tension the critics under-weighted: cold, the fused kernel moves the **least** traffic (0.005 GB, 1 launch) and **still wins** — so the cold data mildly favors full fusion, just for a different reason than unlimited claimed.)*

(One caveat on the number itself: `keyEvidence` reports "final 10.65×," so the tabulated **10** is almost certainly a display cap, not a measured plateau.)

**Verdict:** one clean intrinsic slice (→ Triton, no shared atomics), one search artifact (→ cuda_noptx, missed the *high-occupancy partial-fusion* lever — its skip of full fusion is a defensible cold-bench call), one contested-bench/regime slice (→ tilelang, opposite defensible verdict on full fusion). Reading "10 vs 6.56" as a capability ranking would miss all three distinctions.

### 5.2 `group_norm` — the largest *artifact-dominated* gap, and a harness stall inflating it
`triton 1.354× · tilelang 1.323× · cuda_unlimited 1.281× · cuda_noptx 0.917×` — spread 1.48× *(all four means dragged low by the shared Trial-1 `cudaMalloc` stall; steady-state ≈ 1.53 / 1.50 / 1.44 / 1.0×)*

group_norm is a two-pass reduction-normalization whose naive form pays **3× HBM traffic** (read for stats, re-read for normalize, write) and sits at the ~837 GB/s roofline (~0.9–1.0× vs torch). The breakthrough is **DSL-agnostic**: keep each 32 MB K=4-group chunk **L2-resident** across an interleaved stats→normalize launch pair so the normalize pass re-reads from the 96 MB L2 — collapsing 3× traffic to 2× and hitting ~20–21 ms.

Three DSLs found and cashed it by *different mechanisms*, converging at 1.28–1.35×: Triton via an interleaved two-kernel pipeline with `evict_last`/`evict_first` hints (**winner**, 1.354×); tilelang via a K=4 chunk pipeline whose own tuning discovery was TH=32 single-warp stats blocks (1.323×, edging cuda_unlimited); cuda_unlimited via a host-side C++ chunk loop with fp32 warp-shuffle accumulators (1.281×). **Crucially, the winner here is Triton, not the widest-lever-set DSL** — and cuda_unlimited's inline-PTX streaming stores (its one lever genuinely unique vs the no-PTX siblings) plus its warp-shuffle reduction (*not* unique to any DSL — cuda_noptx used `__shfl_up_sync` on cumsum too) were *both confirmed non-load-bearing* (store policy cs/cg/plain all ~22.3 ms; shuffle alone neutral; the real win was L2 residency + fp32 accumulators, both DSL-agnostic). The intra-cluster order (Triton > tilelang > unlimited) is **noise/mechanism, not depth or lever-set**.

`cuda_noptx` is the lone loser, but not because it never questioned the traffic. It **did** evaluate L2 residency — and *rejected* it, via a flawed occupancy argument that reasoned only about the **fused-persistent** form ("an 8 MB group exceeds shared/registers, so a resident scheme would throttle to ~12 blocks and lose more BW than the 33% it saves"). What it never tested was the **off-diagonal chunked form** — many CTAs cooperating on a K=4 (32 MB) chunk across an interleaved *multi-launch* pair, which keeps the chunk L2-resident *without* throttling occupancy — the exact form its three siblings used, all plain CUDA / no PTX. So this is still a discovery failure (it missed the winning code it could fully express), just a more specific one than "never asked." And the number understates its true standing: with the Trial-1 `cudaMalloc` stall removed, cuda_noptx's clean 3×-traffic result settles to **~parity with torch (~1.0×)**, not the 0.917× loss the mean reports — so 0.917× is emphatically **not** cuda_noptx's ceiling.

### 5.3 `layer_norm` — same artifact, plus a proof that PTX is non-causal
`cuda_unlimited 2.162× · tilelang 2.101× · triton 2.095× · cuda_noptx 1.637×` — spread 1.32×

Structurally identical story to group_norm: M=64 rows × 16 MB each. The naive 3-pass reads x twice from HBM (~3.22 GB); the DSL-agnostic lever is that a single 16 MB row fits the 96 MB L2, so **processing one row per launch** lets the apply pass re-read x from L2, cutting traffic ~3.2 GB → ~2 GB. This is the other pole of the inversion, and the mirror is exact at the **port-parity** level: GAP_ANALYSIS's controlled A/B/C showed layer_norm with **one block per row (64 blocks — the *same* 64-block mapping that starves scatter)** is the **fastest** variant, *beating* the 8192-block split-row form. Why the opposite sign? Because a memory-bound 16 MB row gives each of the 64 blocks' threads ~16,384 *independent* loads — enough memory-level parallelism to saturate HBM even at 6% occupancy — whereas scatter's compute-light one-element-per-thread work leaves 64 blocks unable to fill the SMs. **Same decision (map one row/element unit to a block), opposite outcome, because the regime — not the lever — sets whether 64 blocks is "abundant MLP" or "starved occupancy."** (The deeper-pass 2.1× win then layers L2 residency on top; the occupancy inversion is the port-parity fact, kept distinct here from the traffic-cut lever.) Three DSLs found it and landed within ~3% (2.10–2.16×) via *opposite* mechanisms — and this op contains a lovely sub-divergence that isolates mechanism from lever: **`tilelang` won its 2.10× with a cooperative single-kernel** (`T.sync_grid` keeps the row L2-resident across stats→apply), while **`cuda_unlimited` explicitly tried that same cooperative kernel and reverted it** (192 `grid.sync` barriers cost more than the 128 stream launches they remove → 3.24 ms) — winning its 2.16× with multi-launch instead. Same lever, opposite mechanism, both at ~2.1×.

**Attribution fix (this is the trap the task flags).** A trajectory summary credits cuda_unlimited's ~3% edge over the other two cluster members to its inline-PTX `st.global.cs.v4` streaming store. **Do not.** cuda_unlimited's *own* cross-op ablation lists layer_norm among the ops where "`st.global.cs` streaming store measured equal to a plain float4 store." The specific primary measurement beats the summary: the 2.16/2.10/2.10 cluster is **noise + mechanism difference with PTX confirmed non-causal**. There is no legitimate intrinsic component in the top three.

The *only* real story at layer_norm is again the ~32% drop to `cuda_noptx 1.637×`, which stayed in the 3.26 GB 3-pass model, spent its budget on split-row occupancy (PPR sweep, all worse), ILP=4, TPB, and atomic-vs-direct finalize, and concluded "90% of theoretical BW, at floor." Plain host-side row-blocking, no PTX, never tried → **artifact**.

### 5.4 `gather` — one physics, two equally valid routes, and a search miss at the bottom
`cuda_unlimited 1.508× · triton 1.500× · cuda_noptx 1.327× · tilelang 1.311×` — spread 1.15×

The physics is a random per-row gather over a 32 KB row with fully-coalesced idx/out. The top pair reaches ~1.5× by **two different but equal-value routes** — a nice demonstration that "the winning lever" can be a *class*, not a single primitive:
- **cuda_unlimited (algorithmic):** stage the whole 32 KB x-row in shared memory (coalesced float4 `__ldg` → `__syncthreads` → on-chip random gather → streaming store), converting random DRAM (~50% BW) into sequential DRAM + cheap on-chip reads (~90% BW). 1.508×.
- **Triton (cache-residency):** stay in-kernel and pin the ~4×-reused row cache-resident with `eviction_policy=evict_last`, hitting ~97% of HBM peak. 1.500×. *(ncu: the "~97% of HBM peak" figure is **unsupported** — measured 68.6%, and the kernel is ~11 µs, too short for %-peak to be meaningful. The coalescing win is real and triton is genuinely the fastest gather route by wall-clock, but this specific %-peak number is retracted; ncu cannot see the gather divergence at all — all 4 DSLs read an identical 0.008 GB.)*

The bottom pair are both artifacts of *how* they attacked the same coalescing win:
- **cuda_noptx 1.327× (search):** landed on a strong ILP-above-roofline optimum (2D grid + VEC=4 + NG=4 ILP=16) — a route the tile DSL cannot pin — and it *did* try the winning shared-mem x-row staging, but only in a naive un-vectorized one-block-per-row form that regressed (0.0276 ms), then abandoned it. The lever was in-set, tried, deformed, dropped.
- **tilelang 1.311× (search + small intrinsic tail):** found a real glue lever (delete the `idx.to(int32)` conversion kernel — an extra ~4 MB + launch inside the timed region — for 1.07→1.31×) but its one shared-mem attempt staged the *idx* stream, not the reused x-row, so its barrier serialized phases and it never reached the coalescing win. A genuine JIT per-call launch/sync tax on this ~20 µs kernel is a small real tail on top.

**Verdict:** search-path — the coalescing win was expressible everywhere and found in winning form (whether by shared-mem staging or eviction hint) by the top pair; the bottom pair tried it deformed (cuda_noptx) or staged the wrong stream (tilelang), plus a small tilelang JIT-tax tail. **No PTX credit for the winner** — the gather log runs no plain-vs-`st.global.cs` A/B to isolate any streaming-store contribution, and cuda_unlimited's own cross-op ledger shows that store null everywhere it *was* A/B'd; the decisive lever is DSL-agnostic shared-mem x-row staging (cuda_noptx expressed the same primitive; tilelang could via `T.alloc_shared`). Not a capability ranking.

> **On the "equal value" of the top pair:** the 1.508 ≈ 1.500 tie is in *normalized speedup*, not wall-clock. Triton's underlying min runtime (~0.0113 ms via `evict_last`; the "~97% of HBM peak" gloss is retracted — ncu measured 68.6%, see above) is materially faster than cuda_unlimited's shared-staging route (~0.0173–0.0182 ms) — **and this wall-clock gap is independently confirmed by the P2 A/B (triton 0.0127 ms vs unlimited 0.0182 ms)** — so if anything the cache-residency route is the stronger of the two, which *strengthens* (not neutralizes) Triton's eviction-hint ergonomics point. Read "equal value" as "both cleared the coalescing barrier," not "identical performance."

### 5.5 `swish` — convergence at fusion; a ~4% tail that is NOT G=6 at the top
`cuda_unlimited 2.5256× · triton 2.5253× · cuda_noptx 2.447× · tilelang 2.432×` — spread 1.04×

The entire *large* win is fusion: collapse eager `x*sigmoid(x)` (2 passes, ~32 GB) into one fused read+write (~12.9 GB). **Every DSL captures it and lands 2.43–2.53×** — a convergence regime. The interesting part is the ~4% tail, and the attribution must be handled carefully:

- **The top of the table is a tie reached by different routes.** Triton **2.5253** ≈ cuda_unlimited **2.5256**, and *Triton reverted its ILP unroll* — i.e. it reached the top **without** the G=6 consecutive-group MLP layout. cuda_unlimited did find G=6 (a real +3% to ~851 GB/s in its own copy-ceiling probe), but if that lever explained the *ordering* it would separate cuda_unlimited from Triton, and it does not. So **G=6 does not explain the top of the board**; the autotuner reached the same ceiling by a different path. The honest reading: fusion is the story, and the top pair are co-winners.
- **The bottom two are bottom for different reasons.** `cuda_noptx 2.447×` tried a 2×-consecutive-float4 unroll but stopped before sweeping to G=6 — an *unfinished search* on a lever it could express in plain CUDA. `tilelang 2.432×` (lowest) is the *one* place its layout limitation is intrinsic: `T.Parallel` auto-vectorization will not let it pin the consecutive-group layout at all. But note the **magnitude**: even this genuine codegen ceiling costs only ~4% here — a reminder that "intrinsic" does not mean "large."

## 6. How the trajectory becomes the performance table

Putting §3–§5 together, the table is generated by three composable rules — and the mechanism is different in each regime band:

**Rule A — In no-headroom regimes, the DSL is invisible.** Memory-bound-elementwise (5 activations) and cuDNN-floored (lstm) have a single-point relevant set (a max-width copy; a closed-box recurrence). Every DSL's port reaches it, and *every* off-baseline lever — eviction hints, `__ldcs/__stcs`, PTX streaming stores, ILP unrolls, occupancy sweeps, guard removal — reverts inside the ~2.5–3% noise band. Search *depth* is spent confirming the floor from multiple angles, not moving the number. Result: 6 ops (counted as **two findings**) at 0.97–1.03×, and the widest-lever-set DSL posting the *lowest* lstm number (0.974) is the signature that levers cannot bite here.

**Rule B — In headroom regimes, position in the winning cluster is set by whether the search CROSSED the artifact gap.** Reduction-normalization and index-gather both contain an algorithmic lever worth 1.3–2×. Reaching the winning cluster requires *finding* that lever (L2 residency; shared-mem coalescing) — which is a **search-depth** property, not a lever-set one, because the levers are DSL-agnostic. This is exactly why:
- `cuda_unlimited`, the deepest searcher, is in the cluster on all four (LN 2.16, GN 1.28, gather 1.51, scatter 10) — it re-opens floors and runs empirical traffic probes.
- `triton` is in the cluster wherever its reopening habit + eviction hints apply (GN **winner**, LN 2.10, gather ~1.50) and only falls out where its lever-set truly can't reach (scatter, no shared atomics).
- `cuda_noptx` falls *out* of the cluster on LN (1.64) and GN (0.917) not because it lacks a lever but because it stopped searching — the pure artifact.
- `tilelang` is in the cluster wherever a tile/cooperative primitive expresses the lever (LN 2.10, GN 1.32, scatter 7.63) and falls out where the winning form needed low-level control it can't emit or a search it didn't run (gather 1.31).

**Rule C — Intra-cluster order is noise/mechanism; only a true lever-set ceiling or codegen trap creates a permanent slot.** Once inside the winning cluster, who is 1st vs 3rd is mechanism and clock jitter (group_norm: Triton's eviction pipeline > tilelang's TH=32 stats > cuda_unlimited's C++ loop, all ~1.28–1.35× at the same 2× floor; layer_norm's 2.16/2.10/2.10 with PTX proven non-causal). The **only** places a DSL is *permanently* pinned below the cluster are: Triton on scatter (no shared atomics — intrinsic), and tilelang's soft codegen ceilings (swish G=6 unpinnable; cumsum pipelining unusable) — each worth only single-digit percent.

The composite: **`cuda_unlimited` tops the most ops because it searches deepest and can express every DSL-agnostic lever it finds — not because its exclusive PTX levers do work** (they measure null almost everywhere). `triton` is its near-equal, ahead on group_norm, behind only where shared atomics are mandatory. `tilelang` is a co-winner on reduction-normalization (cooperative grid) with a scattering of small codegen-intrinsic losses elsewhere. `cuda_noptx` looks like the weakest DSL in the raw table but is, on the evidence, a *strong* porter whose two big losses are trajectory accidents on levers it could fully express.

## 7. Honest caveats

1. **These are reasoned roofline floors, not `ncu`-measured — and that is causally central, not boilerplate.** Every "at floor / X% of peak" verdict in this study is an analyst's traffic model plus a bench-min runtime, not a profiler counter. This matters because **the wrong traffic models ARE the mechanism behind the two biggest artifact gaps**: `cuda_noptx` computed "3.26 GB / 3-pass" for layer_norm and "25.77 GB irreducible" for group_norm, *believed its own roofline*, and declared "at floor." The same reasoning that makes these floors convenient is what produced the divergences when a floor was wrong. Treat every floor claim as falsifiable — the study itself falsified several by re-opening (layer_norm and group_norm were *both* declared "3× irreducible, done" before a pure-torch pre-probe overturned it).

2. **A per-DSL number is not a per-DSL ceiling.** The cleanest anchor is lstm: the widest-lever-set DSL (`cuda_unlimited`) posts the *lowest* number on the board's most-floored op (0.974×), pure noise on a projection kernel that is ~0.04% of runtime. If the widest lever-set can post the lowest number, no single number bounds a DSL's capability. The rule generalizes: `cuda_noptx`'s 0.917× on group_norm and 1.64× on layer_norm are *trajectory* results — an identical plain-CUDA program that adds the (PTX-free) L2-reuse chunk loop would join the 1.28–1.35× / 2.10× cluster.

3. **Genuine DSL properties (permanent) vs search artifacts (accidental).** For clarity, the full ledger:
   - **Genuine intrinsic ceilings (lever not in the set):** Triton on scatter (no block-scoped shared atomics — the one large, clean example).
   - **Genuine codegen/dtype traps (forced-slow or unusable):** tilelang's **fp64 accumulator trap** (the original layer_norm 0.65× — fp64 at 1/64 rate on AD102; a *porting* characteristic, fixed to fp32 pre-final so not in the committed numbers, but the largest transient gap in the study and the source of the "keep reduction accumulators fp32 on AD102" lesson); can't pin a consecutive-group per-thread layout (swish, ~4%); `T.Pipelined` silently breaks a loop-carried scan carry (cumsum — removes a lever, but is *not* why tilelang trails there; the cumsum ordering is the shared unfinished-EPT-sweep artifact of §4/§6, not this trap); JIT ~3.3 µs/launch tax on ~20 µs kernels (gather/scatter/lstm-projection); int16 atomics unsupported, cooperative grid-size cap, `T.if_then_else` double-load, tiled-scatter shape-hardcoding (all small).
   - **Search artifacts (lever in the set, not found or found deformed):** cuda_noptx missing L2 residency (layer_norm, group_norm — the two biggest artifact gaps); cuda_noptx tried x-row staging un-vectorized and dropped it (gather); cuda_noptx/triton/tilelang never running the EPT=16 register-direct sweep (cumsum); cuda_noptx stopping before G=6 (swish); tilelang staging idx not the x-row (gather).
   - **Non-causal levers to explicitly NOT credit:** inline-PTX `st.global.cs` streaming stores (systematically tie plain stores under HBM saturation — proven on relu, sigmoid, hardsigmoid, elu, gelu, swish, cumsum, group_norm, layer_norm). Crediting cuda_unlimited's cluster edges to PTX would manufacture a phantom intrinsic advantage; its wins are search depth + DSL-agnostic algorithmic levers.

4. **The `cuda_noptx` ⊂ `cuda_unlimited` correlation is a trap.** `cuda_noptx` is exactly `cuda_unlimited` minus inline PTX, and it loses on the ops where the sibling won by a fused/streaming form (scatter, layer_norm). It is tempting to bill this to "no PTX." **Don't** — on every one of those ops the winning *algorithm* (L2 residency, block-per-row shared packed-64) needs no PTX, and the sibling's own A/Bs show its PTX store policy was immaterial. The correlation is that the PTX-less subset also happened to be the run that stopped searching; the causation is the stopping, not the PTX.

5. **The measurement bench is cold, and one champion result is contested on exactly that basis.** `bench.py` clears 256 MB of L2 before each trial. On scatter, `cuda_unlimited` justified its 10× full-fusion win partly on a warm-L2 "not bandwidth-bound" premise that `cuda_noptx` and `tilelang` independently flagged as invalid under the cold bench — and `tilelang` therefore ranked the same lever *worse* and chose partial fusion. Two of three siblings disputing the winner's decisive move means the 10-vs-7.63 slice should be read as *regime-contested*, not a settled ceiling. (Also: the tabulated "10" is likely a display cap; `keyEvidence` shows 10.65×.)

6. **Noise band and "convergence."** Throughout, "convergence" means all four DSLs within the study's ~±2.5–3% run-to-run band (clock ramp, warmup, min-of-trials wobble ~0.4–0.5 ms). Under that definition, 8 of 12 ops converge (5 activations, lstm, plus swish and cumsum as convergence-with-a-small-tail — cumsum's scan cost is ~0 and it sits at the fixed 8.59 GB copy ceiling, so its 1.19–1.24× band is no-headroom, not "small headroom"). Only 4 ops (layer_norm, group_norm, gather, scatter) diverge beyond noise — and of those, three gaps are dominated by search path and only one *slice* of one op (scatter → Triton) is a clean, permanent DSL ceiling.

7. **The DSL "personalities" split into durable traits and single-run search behavior — don't conflate them.** This is the same discipline as caveat 2, applied to §3. Each (op, DSL) cell is **n=1**: one AKO trajectory. So the personality claims fall into two tiers, and only the first tier is a property of the *DSL*:
   - **Structurally grounded (durable — a real DSL property):** Triton's missing block-scoped shared atomics; tilelang's fp64-rate / `T.Parallel`-layout / `T.Pipelined` / int16-atomic traps and its cooperative-grid primitive; cuda_unlimited's PTX lever-set *and* the "unlimited" tier that licenses deeper search. These answer "characteristic of the DSL."
   - **Search-behavior (accidental — a property of *these runs*, may not replicate):** "who searched deepest," "cuda_noptx confidently floors within its own traffic model and stops early." Re-run with more iterations and cuda_noptx might not stop early. cuda_noptx's personality is the most contaminated of the four — its whole story rests on 2 ops (layer_norm, group_norm), both of which are the search artifacts of caveat 3. **Corollary (actionable):** because those gaps are search artifacts, not ceilings, the three artifact cells — cuda_noptx `layer_norm`/`group_norm` and tilelang `gather` — are **re-optimization opportunities**: re-running them with the already-identified PTX-free lever (L2-resident chunk loop / vectorized x-row staging) would most likely close them into their winner clusters.

8. **A specific measurement artifact and an inherited-provenance caveat.** (a) The **group_norm Trial-1 `cudaMalloc`(8.59 GB) stall** (§1 block-quote) depresses *all four* group_norm means below steady state; the tabulated 0.917/1.281/1.323/1.354 are stall-dragged, and the real cluster/loser standings are ≈ 1.53/1.50/1.44/1.0×. Any statement about the *magnitude* of the group_norm gap inherits this ~10-point inflation (the *relative* ordering is unaffected). (b) This analysis is built on the same primary logs as `RESULTS.md`/`GAP_ANALYSIS.md`, and it inherits their caveat that **their own adversarial review never completed** — the two independent reviewers were "residual-explainers … confirmatory by construction," and the genuinely adversarial passes stalled. Our three critics (intrinsic-vs-artifact, lever-expressibility, regime/numbers) *are* the adversarial pass for *this* document and did complete, but the underlying roofline floors they build on remain reasoned models, not `ncu` counters. Treat "at floor / X% of peak" as falsifiable — this study has already falsified several such floors by re-opening them.
