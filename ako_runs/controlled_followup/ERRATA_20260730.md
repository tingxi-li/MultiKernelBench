## Searcher and native-tier disclosure

The native optimization trajectories were LLM-agent searches, not repeated
language-only experiments. The five compute operations have one Opus 4.8
trajectory per `(operation, DSL)` cell. LayerNorm's searcher and sampling seed
are not attributable from the retained artifacts. A separate Sonnet 4.6
campaign changes some nominally identical cells by 1.7--2.1x. That is evidence
that native gaps are searcher-sensitive; with one run per model/cell, it is not
an estimate of a model-population effect. Sonnet did not record `compute_s` and
must not be pooled into native convergence-time tables.

Native rows used cards on a four-GPU RTX 6000 Ada host with free-running clock
states. Per-row GPU identity is absent from `convergence.csv`, and vendor
identity baselines differ by 27--42% on compute-bound operations. “One GPU”
therefore means one GPU model for the native tier, not one controlled physical
unit. The Phase-1, Phase-2, and follow-up controlled campaigns used pinned,
serialized sessions; native cross-lane runtimes and cumulative compute times
cannot be post-hoc normalized into causal DSL effects.

Sections describing native trajectory shape, time-to-95%, or “meaningful
decisions” must be read as properties of these single agent runs under their
cell-specific prompts. LayerNorm was a hinted calibration; standard matmul and
SDPA also received different priors. They are not DSL convergence laws.

intrinsic language reachability difference. Quote `1.4481937x` as an
implementation/recipe frontier combining realization and recipe reachability,
not a same-epilogue code-generation gap or DSL ceiling.

## Vendor-anchor correction

The historical Phase-2 statement that no matched custom lane beat torch fp16
is true for that historical campaign's values. It does not certify the current
relation. Conversely, comparing the new 1.2897/1.2994 ms custom points directly
with the historical 1.522688 ms torch point does not certify that the vendor
anchor has now been beaten.

The old torch fp16 path casts the activation inside the timed region, casts the
bias to fp16, runs GELU and softmax in the half path, then returns fp32. The
follow-up custom paths receive a precast fp16 activation, and fused-v2 requires
fp32 bias, probability, and output semantics. A closure campaign must measure
both the historical arithmetic with a precast activation and a true
mixed-contract torch path, contemporaneously with the custom cells.

### Same-GPU frontier mechanism closure

The later `fused_frontier_closure_v3` run supersedes cross-card attempts to
quantify how much of the old `1.4481937x` frontier came from the CUDA
shared-memory epilogue barrier.  It completed `120/120` measurements in 15
randomized complete blocks on one physical GPU.

The streamed/old paired ratios were `0.862385` for both retained no-PTX
recipes and `0.789461` for CUDA-unlimited, with exact intervals wholly below
one and within-family Holm-adjusted `p=0.000183105`.  Compiler recipes still
won every fixed comparison: TileLang/Triton had about 20% lower latency than
streamed no-PTX and about 9% lower latency than streamed unlimited.  The fixed
four-recipe within-block spread was `1.250500x`, exact interval
`[1.243647, 1.256544]`.

Therefore `1.4481937x` must not be quoted as a same-recipe code-generation
constant.  Removing the allocation barrier materially improves the CUDA
frontier, but does not eliminate the remaining fixed-recipe compiler/CUDA gap.
The `1.250500x` value is itself scoped to TileLang `g08`, Triton `g05`,
streamed no-PTX `g05`, and streamed-unlimited `g07`; it is not a universal DSL
frontier.  Original frozen-split eligibility was imported for timing, while
the separate fresh stress results retain their own scope.

**Inline robustness and effort warning:** the compiler artifacts failed their
separate fresh-seed stress, and the old/new stress runs used disjoint seeds.
CUDA received an oracle-informed streamed redesign while the compiler artifacts
remained grid-frozen, so `1.250500x` is an asymmetric-engineering snapshot, not
an equilibrium recipe frontier.
